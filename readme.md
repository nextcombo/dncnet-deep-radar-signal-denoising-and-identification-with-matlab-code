# DNCNet: Deep Radar Signal Denoising and Identification [with MATLAB Code]

In the crowded electromagnetic environment, the ability to identify and mark interesting radio spectrum in real-time is a key factor for spectrum monitoring, transmitter identification, mobile transmitter tracking, and cognitive radio or radar applications [1]. In this paper, we focus on the recognition/identification of radar signals, which is highly challenging, such as when data noise is high and the distributions in the training and test datasets are different. Effective and unique signal features are key to signal recognition and validation. Over the years, manual feature extraction methods based on expertise have been used, such as Wigner and Choi-Williams time-frequency distribution-based features [2] and short-time Fourier transform-based features [3]. Features selected from these techniques create extensive separation in the spectral domain, allowing for the successful differentiation of different signals or transmitters. However, these methods generally lack automation and generalizability.
Deep learning has significantly enhanced feature extraction capabilities, not only in computer vision and natural language processing involving image, video, speech, and text data but also in recent years, it has been widely applied in the field of radio frequency devices for analyzing mobile traffic data [4], communication [5], and radar signals. For example, in previous works, such as [6]-[8], raw time-series radar signals are first converted into two-dimensional images through some time-frequency analysis methods, and then preprocessed with cropping, etc., before being input into deep neural networks (DNN). These image-based methods are highly popularized, but they also have some drawbacks. For example, compared to raw time-series data, time-frequency analysis may lead to amplitude information loss; this is inconsistent with the trend of end-to-end learning in deep learning, where models learn all steps from the initial input stage to the final output; converting all original signal samples into time-frequency distribution images is very time-consuming, which may increase the computational burden of end-to-end learning.
In addition to utilizing the pre-processing time-frequency information mentioned above, for example, authors in documents [9]-[11] directly identify radio signals from raw time series. For complex-valued inputs, their real and imaginary parts are combined into a set of 2N vectors, which are then fed into a narrow two-dimensional convolutional neural network (CNN), where N is the length of the signal samples. This demonstrates the effectiveness of CNNs in representing high-level features of radio signal data as well as visual data such as images and videos. In these works, GNU Radio also created a small corpus of standard datasets that include synthetic channel effects and airborne transmission datasets. However, when the test dataset has a different distribution from the training dataset, the performance of pre-trained DNNs may degrade. For instance, due to environmental noise, during combat phases, interference and other disturbances can damage signal quality, reduce the signal-to-noise ratio, and intercepted signals may differ from those in the training dataset. Moreover, numerical experiments show that common CNN classifiers trained on high signal-to-noise ratio datasets suddenly drop accuracy when tested on low signal-to-noise ratio datasets. Therefore, issues and challenges associated with the distribution mismatch between training and testing data are very common in the field of radio signal recognition and urgently need to be addressed.
In this paper, we employ the divide-and-conquer approach (e.g., [13]) to address the aforementioned issues/challenges, namely learning a denoising model to first restore signals and then developing another classifier to identify them. A qualitative analysis indicates that the denoising model can ensure robustness against noisy signals with a wide range of signal-to-noise ratios, thereby maintaining high accuracy for pre-trained DNN classifiers on the test dataset. There is a paucity of literature on denoising based on DNNs, yet we have witnessed the flourishing development of image denoising. The U-Net architecture [14] has been widely applied in model-based image restoration denoising techniques, where downsampling captures context and upsampling operations achieve precise localization. In [15], the residual learning strategy is utilized to incorporate the denoising model into very deep architectures, capable of handling noise levels unknown to Gaussian denoising. The FFDNet proposed in [16] introduces a noise level estimation subnetwork to enhance flexibility in processing spatially varying noise. Work in [17] extends the denoising model to spatial/channel-related and spatial/channel-varying true noise. In [18], the integration of high-level vision tasks with the denoising model is achieved through image semantic guidance to improve the perceptual quality of the denoised images.
The main contributions of this paper are threefold. 1) Considering the application background and natural demand of model-based denoising technology, a radar signal detection and synthesis mechanism is proposed based on intercepted signals and current threat databases that require clean data and noisy data to be paired for learning. 2) The two-dimensional image denoising model is applied to one-dimensional radar signal restoration, introducing a denoising with classification network (DNCNet) and a two-stage training process, enhancing the mapping between the denoising results and the classifier's perceptual representation, thereby further improving classification accuracy. 3) A comprehensive performance verification of the proposed DNCNet algorithm is conducted, comparing it with existing algorithms. In particular, to strengthen validation and comparison, in addition to the widely used additive Gaussian white noise (AWGN) commonly used in literature (e.g., [6]-[8]), it ensures experimental settings closer to real channel and environmental received noise.
Algorithm framework
Figure 1. Radar signal reception and synthesis program. The signal reception unit deinterleaves and estimates parameters for airborne radar signals, outputting labeled true received signals. This unit provides guidance for the signal generator in the signal synthesis unit to produce clean synthetic signals without noise, which are identical to the received signals but labeled. The outputs from these two units form the paired dataset for subsequent DNN algorithms.
Figure 2. Proposed DNCNet structure. The upper subfigure is the first training process, updating the denoising model (in cyan) through joint reconstruction loss and classification loss. The bottom subfigure shows the retraining process after completing the training of the denoising model, where only the parameters in the classification model (in cyan) are optimized.
Figure 3. Noise level estimation subnetwork within the proposed DNCNet. Noisy signals (×2×N) are first fed into this subnetwork. Its corresponding output (×2×N) is concatenated with the original input signal to form new samples (×4×N), which are then fed into the subsequent U-net architecture subnetwork, as shown in Figure 4.
4. Suggested U-Net architecture subnetwork in DNCNet. Its input data is the cascade of raw noise data and the output of the noise level estimation subnetwork shown in Figure 3.
